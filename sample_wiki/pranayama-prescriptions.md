Pranayama (breath regulation) is not generic — classical texts systematize techniques with explicit constitutional and energetic targeting. This page maps eight major pranayama techniques to dosha patterns, [[pancha-kosha|Kosha]] layers, seasonal contexts, and specific outcomes, with contraindications. Forced application on an unsuitable constitution can aggravate rather than balance. This is the technique-level deep-dive companion to [[pranayama-prescriptions]], which provides the seasonal overview.

## 2. The Eight Primary Techniques

### 2.1 Nadi Shodhana (Alternate Nostril Breathing)

**Mechanism:** Balances Ida (left/lunar/cooling) and Pingala (right/solar/heating) [[nadis|channels]]; integrates hemisphere function. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Ratio | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | 1:1:1 | 10 min | Foundational; calms scattered mind, reduces anxiety |
| **Pitta** | 1:2:2 (longer exhale) | 10 min | Left-nostril emphasis; cooling |
| **Kapha** | 1:1:1 | 15 min | Can add dynamic elements; stimulating |

**Seasonal:** Universally balancing all seasons. Pitta season (Sharad): left-nostril emphasis. Vata season (Varsha): daily essential, increase duration. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Kosha:** Primary Manomaya (emotional balance), Vijnanamaya (clarity). Secondary Pranamaya, Anandamaya. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Contraindications:** Active congestion (practice gently or substitute). High Pitta with inflammation (shorter duration). [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Outcomes:** Days: reduced anxiety, improved clarity. 2-4 weeks: emotional stability, improved sleep. 8-12 weeks: HRV improvement, equanimity. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.2 Bhastrika (Bellows Breath)

**Mechanism:** Rapid, forceful inhalation/exhalation through nose (60-180 breaths/min). Intense sympathetic activation followed by parasympathetic rebound. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | **Contraindicated** | Aggravates mobile, erratic nature |
| **Pitta** | **Contraindicated** | Excessive heat generation |
| **Kapha** | **Primary indication** | 1-2 min/round, 3-5 rounds; immediate energization, heat, mental clarity |

**Seasonal:** Vasanta (Spring) daily optimal for Kapha. Avoid Grishma (Summer). Light use in Varsha for Agni stimulation. Avoid Sharad. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Contraindications:** Vata/Pitta constitution, pregnancy, heart conditions, hypertension, anxiety disorders, recent abdominal surgery. Supervision recommended for first sessions. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.3 Shitali and Shitkari (Cooling Breaths)

**Shitali:** Inhale through rolled tongue; exhale through nose. **Shitkari:** Inhale through teeth; exhale through nose. Literal evaporative cooling. 5-10 min. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | **Contraindicated** | Cooling aggravates Vata's cold quality |
| **Pitta** | **Primary indication** | 5-10 min daily; immediate cooling; especially afternoon when Pitta peaks |
| **Kapha** | **Contraindicated** | Cooling opposes warming needs |

**Seasonal:** Grishma (Summer) daily essential for Pitta. Sharad (Autumn) valuable. Other seasons: minimal indication except acute heat. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.4 Bhramari (Bee Breath)

**Mechanism:** Nasal inhalation, exhalation with humming ("mmmmm"). Vibration stimulates pineal gland, vagus nerve. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Duration | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | 5-10 min | Anxiety reduction, mental settling |
| **Pitta** | 5-10 min | Mental cooling, reduced perfectionism |
| **Kapha** | 10-15 min | Mental activation, overcomes sluggish thinking |

**Universally balancing** — one of three safe-for-all practices. Especially valuable during Vata and Pitta aggravation. Lower pitches more grounding; higher pitches more stimulating. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Contraindications:** Tinnitus (may worsen), ear infections, severe migraine predisposition. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.5 Ujjayi (Victorious Breath)

**Mechanism:** Inhale/exhale through nose with slight throat constriction creating "ocean sound." Activates Prana and [[pancha-prana|Udana]] Vayu. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Duration | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | 5-10 min | Stabilizing, warming, focusing |
| **Pitta** | 5-15 min | Focused without excessive heat; combine with longer exhale for cooling |
| **Kapha** | 10-20 min | Stimulating, warming, mentally activating; extend throughout yoga practice |

**Seasonal:** Warming quality helpful in Vasanta and Varsha. Avoid or cool-exhale in Grishma. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Contraindications:** Throat infections, high blood pressure, anxiety with trembling. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.6 Kapalabhati (Skull-Shining Breath)

**Mechanism:** Rapid forceful exhalations through nose (passive inhalation). 120-180 breaths/min. Mechanical sinus clearing; sympathetic activation. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | **Contraindicated** | Excessive force on light system |
| **Pitta** | **Caution** | Heating; avoid or minimal in cool environment |
| **Kapha** | **Primary indication** | 1-3 min/round, 3-5 rounds; immediate mental clarity, sinus clearing |

**Seasonal:** Vasanta peak congestion: daily beneficial. Varsha sinus clearing: moderate. Grishma: avoid. Practice on empty stomach, upright seated. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Contraindications:** Vata constitution, pregnancy, hernia, heart conditions, glaucoma, recent surgery. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.7 Advanced Bhramari with Shanmukhi Mudra

Combine Bhramari with specific finger placement (ears, eyes, mouth) closing "six gates." Profound sensory withdrawal ([[indriya-nigraha|Pratyahara]]). Kosha emphasis: Manomaya → Vijnanamaya → Anandamaya transition. 5-10 min. For experienced meditators. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

### 2.8 Coherent Breathing (6-Breath Protocol)

**Mechanism:** Breathing at 5-6 breaths per minute (5s inhale, 5s exhale). Resonates with natural HRV frequency, activating baroreceptor feedback loop. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

| Dosha | Duration | Best Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | 10-15 min | Afternoon (2-5 PM) or pre-sleep |
| **Pitta** | 10-15 min | When overwhelmed or over-driven |
| **Kapha** | 5-10 min | Mid-morning or afternoon |

**Universally beneficial** — no contraindications. One of three safe-for-all practices (with Nadi Shodhana and Bhramari). [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

**Ratio Progression:** Introductory 1:1 → Intermediate 1:1.5 (longer exhale) → Advanced with retention 1:2:1 at slower rate. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

> BRIDGE: Coherent breathing ↔ HRV resonance frequency training. Lehrer & Gevirtz (2014) demonstrated that breathing at ~5.5 breaths/min maximizes HRV amplitude via baroreceptor-cardiac vagal coupling. [Source: pranayama-prescriptions.md]

## Seasonal Yoga and Pranayama Protocols

Just as diet and lifestyle adapt to the seasons in [[ritucharya]], Yoga asana and Pranayama practice should shift to counteract the dominant Doshic influences of each Ritu (season). For the complete technique-by-technique pranayama prescription guide with dosha indications, contraindications, Kosha mapping, and seasonal schedule, see [[pranayama-prescriptions]]. This is the practice-level application of [[ritu-dosha-mapping]] theory, providing specific movement and breathing protocols for maintaining inner balance year-round. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## Season-by-Season Protocol

### Vasanta (Spring) — Counteract Kapha Sluggishness
**[[asana|Asana]]:** Energising sequences. Sun Salutations (Surya Namaskar), backbends (Bhujangasana/Cobra, Dhanurasana/Bow), dynamic standing poses (Virabhadrasana/Warrior series). Goal: build heat, stimulate circulation, combat lethargy.
**Pranayama:** Kapalabhati (Breath of Fire), Bhastrika (Bellows Breath) — clear congestion, invigorate. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

### Grishma (Summer) — Cool Pitta, Conserve Energy
**Asana:** Cooling, non-strenuous. Chandra Namaskar (Moon Salutations), gentle forward bends (Paschimottanasana), twists (Marichyasana). Avoid intense heat-building Vinyasa.
**Pranayama:** Sheetali (curled tongue cooling breath), Sheetkari (hissing cooling breath), Nadi Shodhana (Alternate Nostril Breathing) for calming. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

### Varsha (Monsoon) — Ground Vata Instability
**Asana:** Grounding, stabilising. Tadasana (Mountain), Vrikshasana (Tree), gentle flows, seated twists. Mindful, slow transitions. Avoid excessive jumping or high-impact movement.
**Pranayama:** Ujjayi (Victorious Breath), Nadi Shodhana — soothe the nervous system. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

### Sharad (Autumn) — Balance Pitta Heat + Vata Dryness
**Asana:** Mixed cooling and grounding. Gentle twists, forward bends (Pitta pacification). Restorative poses like Balasana (Child's Pose) for Vata grounding. Moderate pace.
**Pranayama:** Sheetali if warm; Nadi Shodhana to balance both Doshas. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

### Hemanta (Pre-Winter) — Build Warmth, Use Peak Strength
**Asana:** Warming, dynamic. Sun Salutations, standing poses, gentle backbends. Longer holds to build stability and internal heat.
**Pranayama:** Ujjayi to build internal heat. Surya Bhedana (Right Nostril Breathing) to energise. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

### Shishira (Winter) — Generate Heat, Combat Kapha Accumulation
**Asana:** Heat-building and invigorating. Strong Vinyasa flows, inversions (Sirsasana/Headstand if practiced), backbends. Counter cold and lethargy.
**Pranayama:** Bhastrika and Kapalabhati to generate heat and clear Kapha. Ujjayi throughout practice. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## Kosha Mapping

Seasonal Yoga and Pranayama practices map directly to the [[pancha-kosha]] framework. Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga maps onto kosha development: Asana refines **Annamaya** (physical sheath), Pranayama refines **Pranamaya** (vital energy sheath), Pratyahara marks the **Manomaya-Vijnanamaya** transition, and Dharana/Dhyana develop **Vijnanamaya**. Seasonal practice selection thus targets specific koshas via specific limbs — e.g., vigorous asana in Hemanta builds Annamaya warmth, while cooling Pranayama in Grishma regulates Pranamaya heat. [Source: pancha_kosha_framework.md]
