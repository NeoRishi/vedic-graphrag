The Ritu-Dosha Mapping is the complete reference for how the three doshas ([[vata-dosha|Vata]], [[pitta-dosha|Pitta]], [[kapha-dosha|Kapha]]) cycle through Sanchaya (accumulation), Prakopa (aggravation), and Prashama (pacification) across the six Ayurvedic seasons. This mapping is foundational to [[ritucharya]] practice, providing both the predictive model for seasonal health challenges and the precise timing logic for preventive interventions including Panchakarma. It also documents the [[ritu-dosha-mapping|Adana-Visarga Kala]] (extractive vs. nourishing half-year) framework, Agni variation across seasons, and the clinical significance of timing therapeutic procedures to dosha cycle peaks. The NeoRishi [[vbr-engine|VBR Engine]] uses this mapping as its core seasonal modulation layer. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## The Three-Phase Principle

Each dosha follows a three-phase cycle across the seasons: [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

1. **Sanchaya (संचय, Accumulation):** The dosha quietly builds in the body during a season when environmental conditions match the dosha's qualities
2. **Prakopa (प्रकोप, Aggravation):** The accumulated dosha becomes aggravated and manifests as active health challenges, typically in the season following accumulation
3. **Prashama (प्रशम, Pacification/Resolution):** The dosha resolves and diminishes when environmental conditions shift away from its qualities and proper conduct is maintained

See [[shatkriyakala]] for how Sanchaya-Prakopa maps to the six stages of disease progression (Sanchaya → Prakopa → Prasara → Sthana Samshraya → Vyakti → Bheda). [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## Detailed Dosha Movement Across Seasons

### Vata Dosha Cycle

**Accumulation (Sanchaya): [[grishma-ritu|Grishma]] (Summer)**
- **Why:** Summer's heat, dryness, and intense sun match Vata's dry, mobile, light qualities
- **Manifestations:** Dry skin, slight restlessness, irregular appetite, lighter sleep
- **Prevention:** Increase hydration, grounding practices, cooling foods
- **Duration:** Entire Grishma (May 15 – Jul 15)

**Aggravation (Prakopa): [[varsha-ritu|Varsha]] (Monsoon)**
- **Why:** Atmospheric instability, erratic weather, barometric pressure fluctuations, heavy moisture (suppressing Vata's dry quality but activating its mobile/chaotic nature)
- **Manifestations:** Joint pain, arthritis, bloating/gas, anxiety, insomnia, nervous system dysregulation, circulation problems
- **Peak Intensity:** Mid-to-late Varsha (August-September)
- **Therapeutic Intervention:** **Basti** (medicated enema) — the definitive Vata pacification procedure, most effective when Vata is peaked
- **Duration:** Entire Varsha (Jul 15 – Sep 15)

**Resolution (Prashama): [[sharad-ritu|Sharad]] (Autumn)**
- **Why:** Humidity clears, weather stabilizes; Vata's aggravation naturally begins resolving
- **Signs of Resolution:** Joint pain decreases, anxiety subsides, digestion normalizes
- **Duration:** Early-to-mid Sharad (Sep 15 – Oct 31)

**Stability Phase: [[hemanta-ritu|Hemanta]] through [[shishira-ritu|Shishira]] (Winter)**
- **Why:** Cold, heavy external environment grounds and pacifies Vata completely
- **State:** Vata fully pacified; no Vata-specific health concerns
- **Duration:** Nov 15 – Mar 15

[Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Pitta Dosha Cycle

**Accumulation (Sanchaya): [[grishma-ritu|Grishma]] & [[varsha-ritu|Varsha]] (Summer & Monsoon)**
- **Why:** Summer's external heat and monsoon's continued moisture create conditions for Pitta storage. Pitta is externally expressed in summer (drawn to skin for thermoregulation) but also stored in liver and blood. Monsoon's dampness suppresses burning force, so Pitta accumulates internally
- **Manifestations:** Subtle warmth in liver region, early acidity signs, blood taking on heat quality
- **Duration:** Two seasons (May 15 – Sep 15)

**Aggravation (Prakopa): [[sharad-ritu|Sharad]] (Autumn)**
- **Why:** Clear skies and re-emerging intense sun suddenly activate accumulated Pitta — abrupt and powerful
- **Manifestations:** Acid reflux, heartburn, skin inflammation/rashes, burning urination, eye inflammation, nosebleeds, fevers, irritability, insomnia
- **Peak Intensity:** Early-to-mid Sharad (Sep 15 – Oct 31)
- **Therapeutic Intervention:** **Virechana** (therapeutic purgation) — the definitive Pitta pacification procedure
- **Duration:** Entire Sharad (Sep 15 – Nov 15)

**Resolution (Prashama): Early [[hemanta-ritu|Hemanta]] (Early Winter)**
- **Why:** Temperatures cool and sun intensity diminishes
- **Signs of Resolution:** Acidity cooling, skin inflammation subsides, mood stabilizes
- **Duration:** Early Hemanta (Nov 15 – Dec 21)

**Stability Phase: Late Hemanta through [[shishira-ritu|Shishira]]**
- **State:** Pitta fully pacified; internal Pitta subdued for metabolic purposes
- **Duration:** Late Nov – Mar 15

[Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Kapha Dosha Cycle

**Accumulation (Sanchaya): [[hemanta-ritu|Hemanta]] & [[shishira-ritu|Shishira]] (Early & Late Winter)**
- **Why:** Cold, heavy environmental qualities directly accumulate Kapha in tissues. Therapeutic in Hemanta (strong Agni contains it for building), continuing into Shishira
- **Manifestations:** Stable weight gain, tissue building, immune cell production, slight morning congestion
- **State in Hemanta:** Accumulation is healthy, therapeutic, designed for strength building
- **State in Shishira:** Accumulation continues but subtle Agni suppression begins
- **Duration:** Two seasons (Nov 15 – Mar 15)

**Aggravation (Prakopa): [[vasanta-ritu|Vasanta]] (Spring)**
- **Why:** Spring's warmth liquefies accumulated winter Kapha — automatic and powerful, like snow melting
- **Manifestations:** Congestion, sinus drainage, cough, asthma, allergies, lethargy, heaviness, weight gain, suppressed appetite, low motivation
- **Peak Intensity:** Mid-to-late Vasanta (April-May)
- **Therapeutic Intervention:** **Vamana** (therapeutic emesis) — the definitive Kapha pacification procedure
- **Duration:** Entire Vasanta (Mar 15 – May 15)

**Resolution (Prashama): [[grishma-ritu|Grishma]] (Summer)**
- **Why:** Summer's heat and dryness burn away Kapha's heavy, moist qualities
- **Signs of Resolution:** Congestion clears, energy increases, appetite stabilizes, mind lightens
- **Duration:** Entire Grishma (May 15 – Jul 15)

[Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## Adana Kala vs. Visarga Kala

### Adana Kala (आदान काल, Extractive Phase) — Uttarayana

**Translation:** "Taking away phase" — the environment extracts from the organism. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | Winter solstice (Dec 21) → Summer solstice (Jun 21) |
| **Ritus** | [[shishira-ritu\|Shishira]] → [[vasanta-ritu\|Vasanta]] → [[grishma-ritu\|Grishma]] |
| **Solar Movement** | Sun moves northward; intensity increases |
| **Human Strength (Bala)** | Progressively DECREASES |
| **Agni** | Peaks in Shishira, then gradually declines |
| **Direction of Force** | Extractive — heat and dryness draw moisture and resources FROM the body |
| **Therapeutic Orientation** | Prevention and lightening; supporting through depletion |
| **Common Imbalances** | Vata and Pitta aggravation (Kapha pacification) |

**Why "Extractive":** The sun's northward declination creates increasing day length, solar intensity, environmental heat, and decreasing moisture. The environment "takes" from the organism through perspiration, heat dissipation, and metabolic work. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Visarga Kala (विसर्ग काल, Nourishing Phase) — Dakshinayana

**Translation:** "Releasing phase" — the environment releases nourishment to the organism. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | Summer solstice (Jun 21) → Winter solstice (Dec 21) |
| **Ritus** | [[varsha-ritu\|Varsha]] → [[sharad-ritu\|Sharad]] → [[hemanta-ritu\|Hemanta]] |
| **Solar Movement** | Sun moves southward; intensity decreases |
| **Human Strength (Bala)** | Progressively INCREASES |
| **Agni** | Lowest in Varsha, recovering in Sharad, peaked in Hemanta |
| **Direction of Force** | Nourishing — moisture, coolness, and reduced extraction allow building |
| **Therapeutic Orientation** | Building and consolidation; supporting recovery |
| **Common Imbalances** | Kapha accumulation (managed through proper conduct) |

**Why "Nourishing":** Decreasing day length, solar intensity, and increasing moisture/rain. Moon's influence increases relative to sun. The environment "gives" to the organism through cooling, moisture, and reduced heat stress. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

See [[ritu-dosha-mapping]] for the full energetic framework page. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Clinical Significance of Adana-Visarga Awareness

1. **Treatment Timing:**
   - Vamana (spring cleanse): Adana phase, Kapha Prakopa ([[vasanta-ritu|Vasanta]])
   - Virechana (autumn cleanse): Visarga phase, Pitta Prakopa ([[sharad-ritu|Sharad]])
   - Basti (monsoon cleanse): Visarga phase, Vata Prakopa ([[varsha-ritu|Varsha]])
   - Rasayana (rejuvenation): Visarga phase peak, [[hemanta-ritu|Hemanta]]

2. **Metabolic Expectation Management:**
   - In Adana, strength naturally declines despite correct diet — expected and normal
   - In Visarga, strength naturally increases — expected and optimal

3. **Preventive Strategy Selection:**
   - In Adana: monitor/prevent Vata-Pitta aggravation
   - In Visarga: manage Kapha accumulation while supporting beneficial building

[Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## The Three-Phase Intervention Strategy

### Phase 1: Sanchaya (Accumulation) — Preventive Window

**Timing:** During the season when dosha is silently accumulating. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

**Approach:**
- Dietary choices opposing dosha accumulation
- Lifestyle practices preventing accumulation
- Herbal support mildly opposing dosha without harsh intervention
- **Goal:** Prevent sufficient accumulation that Prakopa becomes severe

**Example — Vata Accumulation ([[grishma-ritu|Grishma]]):**
Increase hydration proactively → use grounding practices → include warming, grounding foods → **Result:** Vata accumulates less; monsoon's Prakopa is milder.

### Phase 2: Prakopa (Aggravation) — Therapeutic Window

**Timing:** During the season when dosha is actively aggravated. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

**Approach:**
- Intense dietary opposition to the aggravating dosha
- Vigorous counter-regulatory lifestyle practices
- **Panchakarma procedures** (most effective and safe when dosha is peaked)
- Herbal therapies directly addressing aggravated state
- **Goal:** Resolve aggravation completely; eliminate pathology before chronicity

**Example — Vata Aggravation ([[varsha-ritu|Varsha]]):**
Intense warm, oily, grounding diet → daily Abhyanga → **Basti** at Vata peak → **Result:** Vata resolves completely; downstream pathology prevented.

### Phase 3: Prashama (Resolution) — Support Window

**Timing:** During the season when dosha naturally begins resolving. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

**Approach:**
- Continue opposing the dosha but with moderation
- Support natural resolution process
- Avoid sudden shifts that could disrupt resolution
- Begin subtle preparations for the next dosha's cycle
- **Goal:** Complete resolution; smooth transition to next season

**Example — Vata Resolution ([[sharad-ritu|Sharad]]):**
Continue some Vata-pacifying diet (though Pitta cooling becomes primary) → avoid sudden cold/light introductions → maintain joint/nerve herbal support → **Result:** Vata resolves completely; winter pacification ensured.

[Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## Seasonal Health Challenges by Dosha

### Vata-Type Challenges (Summer-Monsoon)

- **[[grishma-ritu|Grishma]]:** Dry skin, restlessness, irregular appetite, light sleep (accumulation)
- **[[varsha-ritu|Varsha]]:** Joint pain, arthritis, nervous anxiety, insomnia, constipation-diarrhea alternation, gas, bloating (aggravation — peak severity)
- **[[sharad-ritu|Sharad]]:** Residual joint stiffness, nervous sensitivity (resolution — decreasing)

**Prevention:** Early Vata pacification in late spring → consistent oil massage/warming through summer → maximum Vata support in monsoon (Basti if indicated) → maintain resolved state through autumn. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Pitta-Type Challenges (Summer-Autumn)

- **[[grishma-ritu|Grishma]]:** Internal heat rising, early heartburn, subtle liver sluggishness (accumulation)
- **[[varsha-ritu|Varsha]]:** Continued accumulation, occasional acid reflux, blood heat quality (continued accumulation)
- **[[sharad-ritu|Sharad]]:** Acid reflux, heartburn, skin inflammation, rashes, burning sensations, nosebleeds, eye problems, irritability, insomnia (aggravation — peak)

**Prevention:** Early cooling in spring → continued cooling through summer → aggressive liver support in monsoon → maximum Pitta cooling and Virechana in autumn → maintain through winter. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

### Kapha-Type Challenges (Winter-Spring)

- **[[hemanta-ritu|Hemanta]]:** Healthy accumulation for building (therapeutic)
- **[[shishira-ritu|Shishira]]:** Continued accumulation, subtle appetite suppression, early congestion signs (approaching aggravation)
- **[[vasanta-ritu|Vasanta]]:** Congestion, cough, asthma, allergies, sinus drainage, lethargy, heaviness, low motivation (aggravation — peak)

**Prevention:** Careful management in winter (building while preventing excess) → proactive Kapha reduction in late winter → aggressive reduction through spring (vigorous exercise, light diet, Vamana if indicated) → maintain through summer. [Source: ritu-dosha-mapping.md]

## Dosha Seasonal Cycle — Accumulation, Aggravation, Pacification

Ayurveda teaches that the three Doshas — Vata (वात, ether + air), Pitta (पित्त, fire + water), and Kapha (कफ, water + earth) — are not static in the body but fluctuate through a natural annual cycle of Sanchaya (accumulation), Prakopa (aggravation), and Prashama (pacification) driven by seasonal qualities. Understanding this cycle is the mechanistic basis of [[ritucharya]]. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

| **Pitta** | Fire + Water | Hot, sharp, light, oily | Metabolism, digestion, transformation |
| **Kapha** | Water + Earth | Heavy, slow, cold, oily, smooth | Structure, lubrication, stability |

## The Three-Phase Mechanism

1. **Sanchaya (Accumulation):** Environmental qualities similar to a Dosha cause it to slowly build up in the body. Example: the cold, dry air of late autumn/early winter increases Vata. At this stage, symptoms are subtle — mild dryness, slight restlessness.

2. **Prakopa (Aggravation):** If unchecked, the accumulated Dosha overflows its normal channels and begins causing symptoms. Example: the accumulated Kapha of winter liquefies in spring warmth, causing colds, congestion, allergies, lethargy. This is the danger phase.

3. **Prashama (Pacification):** When environmental qualities shift to oppose the Dosha, or when conscious Ritucharya counter-measures are applied, the Dosha naturally subsides. Example: the heat of summer pacifies Kapha but begins to accumulate Pitta.

**The goal of Ritucharya is to intervene during Sanchaya (accumulation) to prevent Prakopa (aggravation).** [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## The "Like Increases Like" Principle

The fundamental Ayurvedic law underlying this cycle: **qualities that match a Dosha increase it; qualities that oppose a Dosha decrease it.**

- Cold + dry weather → increases Vata (which is cold + dry)
- Hot weather → increases Pitta (which is hot)
- Cold + damp weather → increases Kapha (which is cold + heavy)

Therefore, diet and lifestyle should emphasise **opposite qualities** to the dominant seasonal Dosha. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## Personalisation: Prakriti × Vikriti × Ritu

The general seasonal cycle affects everyone, but impact varies by:

- **Prakriti (innate constitution):** A Pitta-Prakriti person is more vulnerable in Sharad (autumn Pitta aggravation); a Vata-Prakriti person in Varsha (monsoon Vata aggravation)
- **Vikriti (current imbalance):** A person with existing Vata imbalance needs extra Vata-pacification regardless of season
- **Ritu (season):** The environmental baseline affecting all constitutions

True personalisation lives at the intersection of all three. This is the logic NeoRishi's recommendation engine implements. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## Dosha Cycle and the Pancha Kosha Model

## Adana-Visarga Kala — Two Energetic Halves of the Year

The Hindu year is split into two Ayanas (अयन, half-years) based on the Sun's solstice movement, each with a distinct energetic character that governs human vitality, digestive strength (Agni), and the appropriate intensity of diet and lifestyle practices. This framework provides the bioenergetic rationale underlying [[ritucharya]] and is a cornerstone of Ayurvedic preventive medicine. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## The Two Halves

### Adana Kala (आदान काल) — The Taking/Depleting Period
- **Duration:** Mid-January to Mid-July (Uttarayana / Northern Solstice)
- **Ritus:** Shishira (Winter) → Vasanta (Spring) → Grishma (Summer)
- **Character:** The Sun's energy is strong and ascendant. It progressively draws moisture and strength (Bala) from the earth and from human beings. Physical strength and vitality **gradually deplete** across these three seasons.
- **Implication:** Diet and lifestyle should progressively lighten. The heaviest, most nourishing foods are appropriate at the start (Shishira/Hemanta border) and the lightest, most cooling at the end (Grishma peak).

### Visarga Kala (विसर्ग काल) — The Giving/Restoring Period
- **Duration:** Mid-July to Mid-January (Dakshinayana / Southern Solstice)
- **Ritus:** Varsha (Monsoon) → Sharad (Autumn) → Hemanta (Pre-Winter)
- **Character:** The Moon's cooling and nourishing energy dominates. Clouds, rain, and cooler temperatures replenish the earth. Physical vitality **progressively increases**, reaching its peak in Hemanta when Agni (digestive fire) is strongest.
- **Implication:** Diet can progressively become heavier and more nourishing. The weakest digestion is at the start (Varsha) and the strongest at the end (Hemanta).

## Strength and Agni Through the Year

| Period | Season | Bala (Strength) | Agni (Digestive Fire) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Adana | Shishira | Good | Strong |
| Adana | Vasanta | Moderate | Weakening (Kapha suppression) |
| Adana | Grishma | **Lowest** | **Weakest** |
| Visarga | Varsha | Low (recovering) | Very weak (atmospheric acidity) |
| Visarga | Sharad | Medium | Increasing |
| Visarga | Hemanta | **Highest** | **Strongest** |

[Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]

## Why This Matters

The Adana-Visarga framework explains:
- Why Ayurveda recommends **light, cooling foods** in Grishma (peak depletion) and **heavy, warming foods** in Hemanta (peak restoration)
- Why **vigorous exercise** is recommended in Hemanta/Shishira but **contraindicated** in Grishma
- Why [[dinacharya]] practices like Abhyanga oil type should change by season (warming sesame in winter, cooling coconut in summer)
- Why Chaturmas (the four monsoon months) is reserved for spiritual practice rather than worldly ambition — the body is in its weakest recovery phase

> BRIDGE: Adana-Visarga Kala ↔ Modern research documenting seasonal variation in: metabolic rate (higher in winter), cortisol rhythms, immune cell counts, gut microbiome composition, and cardiovascular function. The semi-annual depletion/restoration cycle maps to observed physiological seasonality. [Source: Hindu_Months_Health_and_Wellness_full.md]
