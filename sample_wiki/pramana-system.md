Pramana (प्रमाण, "means of valid knowing") is the foundational epistemological framework from Gautama Akshapada's Nyaya Sutras (c. 2nd century BCE). It establishes four irreducible cognitive channels through which a conscious agent arrives at Prama (valid cognition) about a Prameya (knowable object). The Nyaya Sutras open with the claim that correct understanding of the means of valid knowledge leads to Nihshreyasa (निःश्रेयस, the highest human good) — framing epistemology not as abstract inquiry but as a soteriological project. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

## The Four Pramanas

**Pratyaksha (प्रत्यक्ष, Perception)** — Direct sensory-cognitive contact with an object. Two stages: Nirvikalpaka (indeterminate, non-conceptual awareness) and Savikalpaka (determinate, conceptually structured perception). Valid perception must be non-erroneous, determinate, and produced by contact of a sense organ with its proper object. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

**Anumana (अनुमान, Inference)** — Knowledge derived through a structured logical process. The five-member syllogism: Pratijna (thesis), Hetu (reason), Udaharana (example), Upanaya (application), Nigamana (conclusion). Depends on prior recognition of Vyapti (invariable concomitance). Five types of inferential fallacy (Hetvabhasa) provide rigorous error-checking. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

**Upamana (उपमान, Comparison/Analogical Cognition)** — Knowledge of an unknown entity by recognising its similarity to a known description. Irreducible to either perception or inference alone. The mechanism behind classification by analogy and cross-domain pattern recognition. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

**Shabda (शब्द, Testimony)** — Knowledge from the assertion of a reliable person (Aptavacana). Three reliability conditions: speaker competence, truthfulness motivation, and linguistic clarity. Applies to any reliable verbal communication — expert reports, trustworthy witnesses — not only scripture. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

## Structural Principles

- **Irreducibility:** Each Pramana generates knowledge no other can produce
- **Fallibilism with Validity:** All Pramanas can fail — the system accounts for error (Bhrama) as part of its structure
- **Extrinsic Validity (Paratah Pramanya):** Knowledge doesn't validate itself; truth must be confirmed by subsequent cognitions — pragmatic success, coherence, absence of defeaters
- **Compositional Epistemic Workflow:** The 16 Padarthas of Nyaya Sutra 1.1.1 describe an entire epistemic lifecycle — from means of knowing through doubt, motivation, debate, fallacies, and grounds for defeat [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

## Cognitive Science Mapping

> BRIDGE: Pratyaksha (Nirvikalpaka → Savikalpaka) ↔ Predictive Processing (Friston/Clark) — perception as active prediction + error correction; two-stage theory maps to initial sensory signal → conceptually structured experience [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

> BRIDGE: Anumana (Vyapti + Hetu → conclusion) ↔ Bayesian inference — Vyapti as prior probability distribution; Hetu as new evidence; conclusion as posterior. Hetvabhasa maps to Bayesian reasoning failures [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

> BRIDGE: Shabda (Aptavacana reliability) ↔ Social epistemology (Coady 1992) — testimony as fundamental, irreducible knowledge source. Nyaya established this position two millennia before Western analytic philosophy recognised it [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

> BRIDGE: Nirvikalpaka/Savikalpaka ↔ Kahneman's System 1 / System 2 — but Nyaya adds a normative theory of when each mode is appropriate and a diagnostic for when fast cognition goes wrong [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

## Modern Relevance — Information Crisis

**Misinformation Taxonomy:** Disinformation = competent but deliberately untruthful (sincerity violation); Misinformation = sincere but incompetent (competence violation); Malinformation = truthful but misleadingly contextualised (Tatparya violation). Each requires different epistemic response. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

**Algorithmic Epistemology:** Social media distorts all four Pramanas: perception skewed by curated feeds, inference corrupted by biased samples, comparison distorted by filter bubbles, testimony degraded by engagement-optimisation over speaker reliability. [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

## Application

### Decision-Making Protocol (5 Steps)
1. **Epistemic Audit:** For each belief informing the decision, identify its Pramana source
2. **Condition Verification:** Check whether conditions for valid operation were met
3. **Defeater Search:** Actively seek information that could undermine each belief
4. **Multi-Pramana Triangulation:** For high-stakes decisions, require convergence across multiple Pramanas
5. **Pragmatic Feedback:** Monitor outcomes; update accordingly [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]

### NeoRishi AI Integration
- **Knowledge Provenance Tracking:** Tag every AI output with its epistemic source (training data = Shabda, logical processing = Anumana, pattern matching = Upamana, sensor data = Pratyaksha)
- **User Pramana Profiling:** Assess epistemic tendencies; for Pratyaksha-dominant users, offer experiential demonstrations; for Anumana-dominant, provide logical frameworks; for Shabda-dominant, provide curated expert sources with reliability indicators
- **Validation Architecture:** Implement Nyaya's protocol — source identification, condition checking, defeater scanning, pragmatic feedback [Source: pramana_knowledge_system.md]
