Vikriti (विकृति, "after creation" / "imbalance") is the dynamic, real-time expression of the [[tridosha-overview|three doshas]] in an individual's body and mind — the current state as opposed to the fixed innate constitution ([[prakriti-vikriti|Prakriti]]). While Prakriti is the unchanging blueprint established at conception, Vikriti fluctuates constantly in response to diet, lifestyle, emotions, stress, seasons, and environment. **The entire therapeutic focus of Ayurveda is the identification and harmonization of Vikriti** — guiding the individual back toward their Prakriti baseline. Vikriti is not a flaw but an intelligent feedback mechanism: the body's language for communicating deviation from its natural state of balance. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## The Central Therapeutic Axiom

> **Always treat the Vikriti (current imbalance) while keeping the Prakriti (innate constitution) in mind as the ultimate destination.**

This is the most critical clinical principle in Ayurveda. Treating based on Prakriti alone is fundamentally incomplete and can be harmful. A person with a fiery Pitta Prakriti experiencing a Vata Vikriti (anxiety, coldness, dryness) would be further imbalanced by a cooling Pitta-pacifying diet — it would aggravate the cold, dry qualities of the deranged Vata. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## Vikriti as Feedback, Not Failure

Vikriti should be understood as a vital, intelligent feedback mechanism — not a mark of failure. Physical ailments, emotional turbulence, and spiritual disconnection are precise indicators of where lifestyle, diet, and mental habits have moved out of alignment with one's true nature. The process of identifying and harmonizing Vikriti is therefore a profound practice of self-awareness (Svadhyaya). [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## Vikriti and the Six-Stage Disease Model (Samprapti)

Vikriti develops through the [[shatkriyakala|six-stage disease progression]] (Shatkriyakala):

1. **Sanchaya** (Accumulation) — Dosha accumulates in its primary seat. Detectable as subtle Vikriti changes (mild bloating, slight anxiety, sleep disturbance).
2. **Prakopa** (Aggravation) — Dosha intensifies qualitatively. Dosha-attributable symptoms emerge.
3. **Prasara** (Overflow) — Aggravated Dosha breaches its seat, enters systemic circulation.
4. **Sthanasamshraya** (Localisation) — Dosha lodges in a vulnerable tissue ([[dosha-dushya-sammurchana|Dosha-Dushya Sammurchana]]).
5. **Vyakti** (Manifestation) — Full clinical picture.
6. **Bheda** (Chronicity) — Complications cascade.

**The power of Vikriti assessment is in early detection** — identifying imbalances at Stages 1-2 where simple lifestyle corrections suffice, long before disease manifests. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## Diagnostic Methods for Vikriti Assessment

### Dual Self-Assessment (Prakriti vs Vikriti Questionnaire)

The source provides a structured dual-questionnaire method:
- **Part A (Prakriti)**: Answers based on lifelong, consistent traits during periods of health
- **Part B (Vikriti)**: Answers based on how one has been feeling over the past few months

**Gap Analysis**: Compare Prakriti score to Vikriti score. The dosha that has increased most from its baseline is the primary therapeutic target. Example: Prakriti V7/P2/K1 + Vikriti V4/P5/K1 → primary Pitta Vikriti requiring Pitta pacification. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

This dual-questionnaire approach embodies the yogic principle of Pratipaksha Bhavana (contemplating opposites) — comparing one's ideal state to current reality reveals the healing path. It maps directly to NeoRishi's [[prakruti-scan|Prakruti Scan™]] Section 3 (Vikriti assessment). [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

### Tongue Diagnosis (Jihva Pariksha)

The tongue is a mirror of internal organs and digestive health. Daily morning observation (before eating/drinking) is a powerful self-diagnostic tool.

**Tongue Map:**
- Front 1/3 → Heart, chest, lungs
- Central 1/3 → Liver, spleen, stomach, pancreas
- Rear 1/3 → Small and large intestines

**Color Interpretation:**
| Color | Indicates |
|---|---|
| Pale | Kapha imbalance or Rakta Dhatu deficiency |
| Reddish/scarlet | Pitta imbalance, excess heat |
| Black/brown/purplish | Vata derangement |

**Coating (Ama Indicator):**
| Coating | Indicates |
|---|---|
| Thin, clear/white | Healthy |
| Thick white | Kapha imbalance, Ama in Kapha sites |
| Yellow/green | Pitta imbalance, Ama in Pitta sites |
| Brown/black | Vata imbalance, Ama in Vata sites |

**Shape and Markings:**
- Fine cracks → Chronic Vata imbalance, stress
- Deep center crack → Stress held in the spine
- Teeth marks (scalloped edges) → Nutrient malabsorption
- Very smooth, red, wet ("movie star tongue") → Excess Pitta (acidity retracts taste buds)
- Swollen/puffy → Fluid retention, lymphatic stagnation, Kapha imbalance

[Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

### Pulse Diagnosis (Nadi Pariksha)

The ancient art of reading the pulse for a comprehensive picture of doshic state. Three fingers on the radial artery (right wrist for males, left for females):

| Finger | Dosha | Pulse Quality (Gati) | Movement Analogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Index | Vata | Irregular, thin, feeble, fast | Snake (Sarpa Gati) |
| Middle | Pitta | Active, prominent, forceful, jumpy | Frog (Manduka Gati) |
| Ring | Kapha | Slow, broad, deep, wavy | Swan (Hamsa Gati) |

Additional parameters: rate (Vega), rhythm, strength (Bala), width (Sthana). Mental states affect the pulse: worry/fear → weak (Ksheena), anger → rapid (Vegavat).

> BRIDGE: Clinical studies are underway to integrate traditional pulse assessment with Pulse Plethysmography (PPG) sensors to create AI-based models for predicting Vikriti status. Research is also correlating Vikriti assessments with biomarkers like inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6), lipid profiles, and cardiovascular risk factors. ## Critical Clinical Insight: Vata-as-Kapha Mimicry

A physical symptom appearing as classic Kapha imbalance (weight gain, fluid retention, lethargy) may in fact be a **compensatory reaction to deep-seated Vata imbalance**. A person experiencing chronic stress, anxiety, and fear (Vata qualities) may find their body creating a protective shield of heaviness and stability to ground the excessive, uncontained movement.

**Danger**: Treating apparent Kapha symptoms with stimulating and lightening therapies would be counterproductive — it would further aggravate the underlying Vata, worsening the root cause. The correct path is to address the root Vata Vikriti with grounding, warming, and stabilizing practices, allowing the body to naturally release the protective accumulated Kapha.

This demonstrates the necessity of looking beyond surface symptoms to understand the true elemental nature of an imbalance. The subtle elements (Air/Ether → Vata) can masquerade through compensatory expression of gross elements (Water/Earth → Kapha). [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## The Balancing Toolkit: Harmonizing Vikriti

### Dinacharya (Daily Routine) — Foundational Self-Care

Aligning daily activities with the natural [[dinacharya|dosha-clock rhythms]] creates stability and predictability for the body's regulatory systems. An irregular routine is a primary cause of Vata aggravation.

**Dosha-Time Periods** (two 12-hour cycles):
- 6 AM – 10 AM / 6 PM – 10 PM: **Kapha time** (heavy, slow, stable)
- 10 AM – 2 PM / 10 PM – 2 AM: **Pitta time** (hot, sharp, metabolic)
- 2 PM – 6 PM / 2 AM – 6 AM: **Vata time** (light, mobile, active)

Key timing principles: wake before 6 AM (Vata period lightness aids rising), main meal at noon (peak Pitta Agni), bed before 10 PM (avoid Pitta "second wind"). See [[dinacharya]] for the complete step-by-step framework. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

### Ahara (Diet) — Nourishment as Medicine

Diet is the most powerful daily tool for influencing Vikriti, built on the [[shad-rasa|Shad Rasa (six tastes)]] framework. The principle: select foods with qualities **opposite** to the imbalanced dosha.

| Vikriti | Qualities to Counter | Favored Tastes | Key Foods | Foods to Reduce |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vata** (cold, dry, light) | Warm, moist, heavy | Sweet, Sour, Salty | Warm soups/stews, cooked grains, root vegetables, ghee, sesame oil, ginger, cinnamon | Raw salads, crackers, dried fruit, carbonated drinks, large beans |
| **Pitta** (hot, sharp, oily) | Cool, mild, dry | Sweet, Bitter, Astringent | Cucumber, leafy greens, melons, coconut, basmati rice, fennel, mint, cilantro | Hot/spicy food, fermented foods, alcohol, nightshades, excess sour/salty |
| **Kapha** (heavy, cold, moist) | Light, warm, dry | Pungent, Bitter, Astringent | Leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables, quinoa, barley, lentils, ginger, black pepper, turmeric | Heavy/greasy food, dairy (esp. cheese/yogurt), sweet/fatty foods, excess nuts/oils |

**Mindful eating** is essential: eat in calm settings, chew thoroughly, attend to hunger/satiety signals. [Source: Vikriti_Holistic_Personalized_Wellbeing_full.md]

## Prakriti vs Vikriti — Constitution and Current State Overview

Prakriti (innate constitution) and Vikriti (current state of imbalance) form the diagnostic foundation of Ayurvedic assessment. Prakriti is the unique, fixed combination of Vata, Pitta, and Kapha established at conception — an individual's health blueprint. Vikriti is the dynamic, clinically actionable deviation from that blueprint. **Treatment targets Vikriti, not Prakriti.** A common misinterpretation in popular Ayurveda is to treat based on Prakriti alone — this is fundamentally incomplete. [Source: Tridosha_Principle_Comprehensive_Guide.md, dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Prakriti — The Blueprint

Prakriti represents the innate Dosha ratio, established at conception and unchanging throughout life. Seven classical types:
1. **Vata** dominant
2. **Pitta** dominant
3. **Kapha** dominant
4. **Vata-Pitta** dual
5. **Vata-Kapha** dual
6. **Pitta-Kapha** dual
7. **Sama** (balanced tri-Dosha) — rare

**Prakriti determines:**
- Physical build, skin/hair type, metabolic rate
- Mental tendencies (learning style, memory, decision-making)
- Emotional responses and stress patterns
- Inherent strengths and vulnerabilities
- Baseline tissue vulnerabilities (e.g., Vata Prakriti → inherently weaker Asthi, Majja, Shukra Dhatus)
- Which Doshas are most easily aggravated (your dominant Dosha is your most reactive Dosha)
- Optimal diet, exercise, sleep, and seasonal regimen

[Source: Tridosha_Principle_Comprehensive_Guide.md, dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Vikriti — The Current Reality

Vikriti is the present Dosha deviation from Prakriti baseline — the dynamic, clinically relevant variable. It fluctuates based on diet, lifestyle, emotions, environment, age, and season.

- When Vikriti aligns with Prakriti → optimal health
- When Vikriti significantly deviates → symptoms and disease
- **Critical distinction**: A Kapha-Prakriti person CAN have a Vata-Vikriti (from excessive travel, irregular meals, stress) → they present with Vata symptoms (anxiety, insomnia) despite their Kapha nature

**Vikriti determines:**
- Current therapeutic priority (which Dosha to address first)
- Whether the imbalance is same-direction (same-Dosha Vikriti — deeper, harder to treat) or cross-direction (cross-Dosha Vikriti — lifestyle-induced, more responsive)
- All immediate dietary, lifestyle, and herbal recommendations

[Source: Tridosha_Principle_Comprehensive_Guide.md, dosha_dushya_framework.md]
