Shad Darshana (षड् दर्शन, "six viewpoints") refers to the six orthodox (Astika) philosophical systems of Indian thought that accept the authority of the Vedas. These six schools collectively address every major domain of inquiry — epistemology, ontology, cosmology, psychology, hermeneutics, and metaphysics — and form the intellectual foundation upon which Ayurveda, Yoga, Jyotisha, and NeoRishi's frameworks are built. [Source: Samkhya.md] [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md] [Source: Nyaya.md] [Source: Vaisheshika.md] [Source: Mimamsa.md] [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

## The Three Paired Traditions

The six schools form three natural pairs, each coupling a theoretical framework with its practical or investigative complement:

1. **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** (logic + ontology) — How do we know, and what exists?
2. **Samkhya-Yoga** (theory + practice) — What is reality's structure, and how do we transform through it?
3. **Mimamsa-Vedanta** (ritual + metaphysical culmination) — How do we act rightly, and what is ultimate reality?

## Samkhya Darshana — Enumeration Philosophy

Samkhya (सांख्य, "enumeration") is the oldest systematic philosophy in India, founded by Sage Kapila. It provides a dualistic framework distinguishing Purusha (पुरुष, pure consciousness) from Prakriti (प्रकृति, primordial matter), and enumerates the 25 Tattvas (तत्त्व, "principles") through which unmanifest nature evolves into the manifest world. Samkhya is the direct philosophical foundation for Ayurveda's Tridosha system and NeoRishi's constitution-based personalisation. [Source: Samkhya.md]

## Core Duality: Purusha and Prakriti

- **Purusha** — Pure consciousness, the witness (Sakshi), unchanging, infinite, beyond causation. Purusha does not act but illuminates all experience. [Source: Samkhya.md]
- **Prakriti** — Primordial matter, the source of all creation. Prakriti is composed of three Gunas (गुण, "fundamental qualities") and is the active, creative principle. [Source: Samkhya.md]

The interplay between passive Purusha and active Prakriti sets the entire manifest universe into motion. Liberation (Kaivalya, कैवल्य) arises through Viveka (विवेक, "discriminative knowledge") — the clear seeing that Purusha is distinct from Prakriti. [Source: Samkhya.md]

## The 25 Tattvas (Evolution Chain)

The evolution proceeds from the unmanifest to the manifest:

1. **Prakriti** (primordial nature)
2. **Mahat / Buddhi** (महत् / बुद्धि, cosmic intelligence / individual intellect)
3. **Ahamkara** (अहंकार, ego-sense / sense of "I")
4. **Manas** (मनस्, mind / processing faculty)
5-9. **Five Jnanendriyas** (ज्ञानेन्द्रिय, sense organs): hearing, touch, sight, taste, smell
10-14. **Five Karmendriyas** (कर्मेन्द्रिय, action organs): speech, grasping, locomotion, excretion, reproduction
15-19. **Five Tanmatras** (तन्मात्र, subtle elements): sound, touch, form, taste, smell
20-24. **Five Mahabhutas** (महाभूत, gross elements): Akasha (space), Vayu (air), Agni (fire), Jala (water), Prithvi (earth)
25. **Purusha** (pure consciousness)

## Triguna: The Three Fundamental Qualities

Prakriti is composed of three inseparable Gunas that exist in dynamic equilibrium:

- **Sattva** (सत्त्व) — Illumination, clarity, lightness, knowledge
- **Rajas** (रजस्) — Activity, passion, movement, desire
- **Tamas** (तमस्) — Inertia, darkness, heaviness, ignorance

When the three Gunas are in perfect balance, Prakriti remains unmanifest. Disturbance of this equilibrium triggers the evolution of the 25 Tattvas. Every manifest entity — including human constitution — reflects a unique ratio of these three qualities. [Source: Samkhya.md]

## Foundation for Ayurveda

Samkhya's Pancha Mahabhuta (five elements) directly generate the Tridosha:

| Dosha | Elements | Guna Dominance |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| [[vata-dosha\|Vata]] | Vayu (air) + Akasha (space) | Rajas |
| [[pitta-dosha\|Pitta]] | Agni (fire) + Jala (water) | Sattva |
| [[kapha-dosha\|Kapha]] | Jala (water) + Prithvi (earth) | Tamas |

The Prakriti (innate constitution) assessment used in Ayurveda and NeoRishi is fundamentally a Samkhya-based analysis — determining the predominant Guna-element combination in an individual. [Source: Samkhya.md]

NeoRishi's [[vbr-engine]] draws directly on Samkhya's systematic categorisation. The 25 Tattvas map to different layers of human experience measured across the [[pkpf-framework]]. The [[prakriti-vikriti]] assessment module operationalises Samkhya's principle that every individual reflects a unique configuration of Gunas and elements, making personalised wellbeing recommendations possible. The [[pancha-kosha]] model used in NeoRishi's consciousness architecture also traces its ontological roots to Samkhya's layered reality. [Source: Samkhya.md]

## Modern Science Bridges

> BRIDGE: Samkhya's model of emergent complexity (unmanifest Prakriti evolving into increasingly differentiated forms) parallels modern systems theory and complexity science, where simple rules generate complex emergent phenomena.

> BRIDGE: The Purusha-Prakriti distinction maps structurally to the "hard problem of consciousness" in modern philosophy of mind — the gap between subjective experience (Purusha/qualia) and objective physical processes (Prakriti/neural correlates).

> BRIDGE: Samkhya's Triguna model finds echoes in personality psychology's trait dimensions and in the three-factor models of temperament (approach, avoidance, regulation).

## Yoga Darshana — Union Through Practice

Yoga Darshana (योग दर्शन, "philosophy of union"), codified by Sage Patanjali in the Yoga Sutras (~200 BCE), is the practical complement to [[shad-darshana]]. While Samkhya provides the theoretical map of consciousness and matter, Yoga provides the methodology for transformation. Patanjali defines Yoga as "Yogash Chitta Vritti Nirodhah" — the cessation of the fluctuations of the mind-stuff (Chitta). This school directly informs NeoRishi's breathing, meditation, and quest architecture. [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Core Definition

"Yogash Chitta Vritti Nirodhah" (योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः) — Yoga is the restraint (Nirodha) of the modifications (Vritti) of the mind-field (Chitta). When this is achieved, the seer (Purusha) rests in its own nature; otherwise, it identifies with the mental modifications. [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Ashtanga Yoga: The Eight Limbs

Patanjali's systematic path unfolds through eight progressive limbs (Anga):

1. **Yama** (यम, "restraints") — Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha
2. **Niyama** (नियम, "observances") — Shaucha, Santosha, Tapas, Svadhyaya, Ishvara Pranidhana
3. **Asana** (आसन, "posture") — Steady and comfortable seat for meditation
4. **Pranayama** (प्राणायाम, "breath regulation") — Control of Prana through breathing techniques
5. **Pratyahara** (प्रत्याहार, "sense withdrawal") — Drawing the senses inward
6. **Dharana** (धारणा, "concentration") — Fixing the mind on a single point
7. **Dhyana** (ध्यान, "meditation") — Sustained, unbroken flow of attention
8. **Samadhi** (समाधि, "absorption") — Complete union of meditator, meditation, and object

The first five limbs are considered external (Bahiranga) practices; the last three form the internal (Antaranga) practices collectively called Samyama (संयम). [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Five Chitta Vrittis (Mental Modifications)

Patanjali classifies all mental activity into five types:

1. **Pramana** (प्रमाण) — Valid cognition (through perception, inference, or testimony)
2. **Viparyaya** (विपर्यय) — Misconception / erroneous knowledge
3. **Vikalpa** (विकल्प) — Imagination / verbal conceptualisation without real object
4. **Nidra** (निद्रा) — Sleep (a modification supported by the absence of content)
5. **Smriti** (स्मृति) — Memory (retention of past experiences)

Each Vritti can be either Klishta (painful/afflicted) or Aklishta (non-painful/non-afflicted). [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Five Kleshas (Afflictions)

The root causes of suffering that Yoga aims to eliminate:

1. **Avidya** (अविद्या, "ignorance") — The root Klesha; mistaking the non-eternal for eternal
2. **Asmita** (अस्मिता, "ego-sense") — Confusing Purusha with the instruments of knowing
3. **Raga** (राग, "attachment") — Clinging to pleasure
4. **Dvesha** (द्वेष, "aversion") — Repulsion from pain
5. **Abhinivesha** (अभिनिवेश, "fear of death") — Clinging to embodied existence

Avidya is the soil from which the other four Kleshas grow. [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Ishvara Pranidhana

Ishvara Pranidhana (ईश्वर प्रणिधान, "surrender to the supreme consciousness") appears both as a Niyama and as an independent path to Samadhi. Patanjali defines Ishvara as a special Purusha untouched by Kleshas, Karma, and their residues. The sacred syllable OM (Pranava) is Ishvara's designator. [Source: Yoga_Darshana.md]

## Application

NeoRishi's [[spanda|SPANDA Quest]] structures its transformative journey along Patanjali's eight limbs, with each phase corresponding to progressively deeper engagement. The [[vbr-engine]] integrates Pranayama and Dhyana timing based on Panchang data, aligning breath practices with optimal circadian and lunar windows. > BRIDGE: The Chitta Vritti model anticipates modern cognitive psychology's classification of mental processes — perception, misperception, imagination, sleep states, and memory are all recognised categories in contemporary cognitive science.

> BRIDGE: Pranayama's effects on autonomic nervous system regulation are validated by research on vagal tone and heart rate variability (HRV). Slow breathing techniques shift the sympathetic-parasympathetic balance toward rest-and-digest states.

> BRIDGE: The Klesha framework maps to cognitive-behavioural therapy's identification of cognitive distortions — Avidya as fundamental attribution error, Raga/Dvesha as approach/avoidance biases, Asmita as ego-centric bias.

> BRIDGE: Dhyana (meditation) practices show measurable effects on cortical thickness, default mode network activity, and stress biomarkers (cortisol, inflammatory cytokines) in neuroimaging and psychoneuroimmunology research.

## Nyaya Darshana — Logic and Epistemology

Nyaya (न्याय, "method of logical reasoning"), founded by Sage Gautama (also called Akshapada), is the Indian school of logic and epistemology. It establishes the four valid means of knowledge (Pramana) and provides the formal logical framework — including a rigorous five-member syllogism — that underpins all Indian philosophical debate. Nyaya's systematic approach to valid reasoning directly inspires NeoRishi's inference-based personalisation engine. [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Four Pramanas (Means of Valid Knowledge)

Nyaya accepts four Pramanas (प्रमाण, "means of right knowledge"):

1. **Pratyaksha** (प्रत्यक्ष, "perception") — Direct sensory contact with an object. Subdivided into Nirvikalpaka (indeterminate, pre-conceptual) and Savikalpaka (determinate, conceptualised).
2. **Anumana** (अनुमान, "inference") — Knowledge derived from logical reasoning based on prior perception. Requires a Vyapti (invariable concomitance) between the middle term (Hetu) and the major term (Sadhya).
3. **Upamana** (उपमान, "comparison") — Knowledge gained by recognising similarity between a known and an unknown object based on a reliable description.
4. **Shabda** (शब्द, "testimony") — Knowledge from the statement of a trustworthy person (Aptavachana). Includes both Vedic (Shruti) and worldly (Laukika) testimony.

The Nyaya epistemological framework later influenced Mimamsa (which added two more Pramanas) and was debated extensively by Vedanta and Buddhist logicians. [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Five-Member Syllogism (Nyaya Anumana)

Nyaya formalises deductive reasoning through a five-step argument:

1. **Pratijna** (प्रतिज्ञा, "proposition") — The thesis to be proved. Example: "The hill has fire."
2. **Hetu** (हेतु, "reason") — The logical ground. Example: "Because there is smoke."
3. **Udaharana** (उदाहरण, "example") — A universal rule illustrated by example. Example: "Wherever there is smoke, there is fire, as in a kitchen."
4. **Upanaya** (उपनय, "application") — Applying the rule to the case. Example: "The hill has smoke."
5. **Nigamana** (निगमन, "conclusion") — Restating the established thesis. Example: "Therefore, the hill has fire."

This structure is more elaborate than the Western Aristotelian syllogism (which uses three terms), deliberately incorporating the example and application steps to ground abstract reasoning in observable reality. [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Sixteen Padarthas

Nyaya enumerates 16 Padarthas (पदार्थ, "categories") as the complete set of objects of right knowledge and logical discourse. These include the four Pramanas, Prameya (objects of knowledge), Samsaya (doubt), Prayojana (purpose), Drishtanta (example), Siddhanta (established doctrine), Avayava (members of syllogism), Tarka (hypothetical reasoning), Nirnaya (determination), Vada (honest debate), Jalpa (sophistic debate), Vitanda (destructive debate), Hetvabhasa (fallacious reasons), Chala (equivocation), Jati (futile objections), and Nigrahasthana (points of defeat). [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Relationship with Vaisheshika

Nyaya and [[shad-darshana]] form a complementary pair: Nyaya provides the epistemological method (how we know), while Vaisheshika provides the ontological categories (what exists). Historically, these two schools merged into the Nyaya-Vaisheshika synthesis, with Nyaya's logic applied to Vaisheshika's categorical framework. [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Application

NeoRishi's Tarka Engine is directly inspired by Nyaya's logical framework. The inference chains that drive personalised recommendations follow a structure analogous to Nyaya's Anumana: observed data (Pratyaksha of user inputs) combined with established rules (Vyapti from Ayurvedic principles) produce inferred conclusions (personalised dosha-based recommendations). The five-member syllogism model can be seen operating when the [[vbr-engine]] reasons: "This user needs cooling practices (Pratijna), because their Pitta is elevated (Hetu), and elevated Pitta is balanced by cooling foods and routines as documented in classical texts (Udaharana)..." [Source: Nyaya.md]

## Modern Science Bridges

> BRIDGE: Nyaya's Anumana (inference based on invariable concomitance / Vyapti) shares deep structural parallels with Bayesian reasoning — both involve updating beliefs based on observed evidence and known conditional relationships.

> BRIDGE: The five-member syllogism anticipates formal logic and scientific method: hypothesis (Pratijna), evidence (Hetu), generalisation (Udaharana), application (Upanaya), and conclusion (Nigamana) mirror the structure of empirical reasoning.

> BRIDGE: Nyaya's classification of logical fallacies (Hetvabhasa) — including Asiddha (unestablished), Viruddha (contradictory), and Savyabhichara (irregular) — parallels modern informal logic's taxonomy of fallacies.

## Vaisheshika Darshana — Atomism and Categories

Vaisheshika (वैशेषिक, "science of particularity") is one of the six orthodox Darshanas, founded by Sage Kanada. It provides a systematic atomistic ontology — classifying all reality into six (later seven) fundamental categories (Padarthas) and proposing that all matter is composed of indivisible atoms (Paramanu). Paired with [[shad-darshana]] (logic + ontology), Vaisheshika supplies the categorical framework while Nyaya supplies the epistemological method. [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

## Core Principles

### Six Padarthas (Categories of Reality)

| Padartha | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Dravya** (द्रव्य) | Substance | Earth, water, fire, air, space, time, direction, atman, manas |
| **Guna** (गुण) | Quality | Colour, taste, smell, touch, number, dimension |
| **Karma** (कर्म) | Action/motion | Upward, downward, contraction, expansion, locomotion |
| **Samanya** (सामान्य) | Generality/universal | "Cow-ness" shared by all cows |
| **Vishesha** (विशेष) | Particularity | What makes each atom unique — the school's namesake |
| **Samavaya** (समवाय) | Inherence | The inseparable relation between substance and quality |

[Source: Vaisheshika.md]

### Paramanu Theory (Atomism)

Atoms (Paramanu) of the four material elements — Prithvi (earth), Jala (water), Agni (fire), Vayu (air) — combine in dyads and triads to form all perceivable matter. Akasha (space) is non-atomic and all-pervading. This is one of the earliest systematic atomic theories in world philosophy, predating Democritus. [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

### Nine Dravyas (Substances)

The five Mahabhutas (Prithvi, Jala, Agni, Vayu, Akasha) plus four non-material substances: Kala (time), Dik (direction/space), Atman (self), and Manas (mind). This nine-fold classification covers all possible substrates of reality. [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

## Application

Vaisheshika's systematic categorisation philosophy directly informs how NeoRishi structures its [[neorishi-archetypes|archetype system]] — classifying users across multiple dimensions (Dosha, Guna, life-stage, goals) with precise quality markers, analogous to how Vaisheshika categorises all reality through Padarthas. The Guna-Dravya-Karma triad (quality inhering in substance, expressed through action) mirrors the Prakriti-Kosha-Pillar mapping in [[pkpf-framework]]. [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

## Modern Science Bridges

> BRIDGE: Paramanu Theory ↔ Atomic theory in modern chemistry — independent convergence on the idea that matter has discrete, fundamental building blocks [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

> BRIDGE: Six Padarthas as categorical ontology ↔ Category theory in mathematics and formal ontology in computer science — both seek irreducible categories for classifying all entities and relations [Source: Vaisheshika.md]

## Mimamsa Darshana (Purva Mimamsa) — Vedic Hermeneutics

Mimamsa (मीमांसा, "investigation/inquiry"), specifically Purva Mimamsa ("earlier investigation"), is the orthodox Darshana of Vedic hermeneutics founded by Sage Jaimini. It focuses on the correct interpretation of Vedic rituals and duties, establishing Dharma (righteous action) as the supreme value and developing sophisticated rules for textual interpretation. Paired with [[shad-darshana]] (Uttara Mimamsa, "later investigation"), Mimamsa addresses the Karma-Kanda (action portion) of the Vedas while Vedanta addresses the Jnana-Kanda (knowledge portion). [Source: Mimamsa.md]

## Core Principles

**Dharma as Central Concept** — Right action according to Vedic injunction. Mimamsa defines Dharma not through metaphysical speculation but through careful analysis of what the texts command, and asserts that correct ritual action is the path to cosmic harmony. [Source: Mimamsa.md]

**Apurva (अपूर्व)** — The unseen moral potency generated by righteous action. When a ritual is performed correctly, it generates an invisible causal force (Apurva) that produces the desired result at the appropriate future time. This is the mechanism of Karma before the concept was fully systematized — action generates consequences through a non-arbitrary causal structure. [Source: Mimamsa.md]

**Six Pramanas** — Mimamsa expands Nyaya's four means of valid knowledge by adding Arthapatti (अर्थापत्ति, presumption — knowledge through necessary postulation) and Anupalabdhi (अनुपलब्धि, non-apprehension — knowledge through the demonstrable absence of something). This expanded epistemology reflects Mimamsa's need for more interpretive tools when dealing with complex textual meaning. [Source: Mimamsa.md]

**Intrinsic Validity (Svatah Pramanya)** — Unlike Nyaya's extrinsic validity, Mimamsa holds that a cognition is presumed valid unless defeated. This has profound implications: it means the Vedic injunction carries authority by default, requiring a positive reason to doubt rather than a positive reason to believe. [Source: Mimamsa.md]

**Ritualistic Duty** — Daily, seasonal, and life-stage rituals are not optional devotional acts but structural obligations that maintain cosmic and social order. This resonates with [[rta-cosmic-order]]'s principle that order requires active, conscious participation (Yajna). [Source: Mimamsa.md]

## Application

## Vedanta Darshana (Uttara Mimamsa) — Ultimate Reality

Vedanta (वेदान्त, "end/culmination of the Vedas"), also known as Uttara Mimamsa ("later investigation"), is the most influential of the six orthodox Darshanas. Founded on the Prasthana Traya (प्रस्थान त्रय, "triple foundation") — the Upanishads, Brahma Sutras, and Bhagavad Gita — its central teaching concerns the relationship between Atman (individual self) and Brahman (universal consciousness). Vedanta provides the metaphysical ground for NeoRishi's entire consciousness architecture. [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

## Three Key Schools

| School | Founder | Core Position | Atman-Brahman Relation |
|--------|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Advaita** (अद्वैत) | Shankaracharya (8th c.) | Non-duality: Brahman alone is real; the world is Maya | Absolute identity — Atman *is* Brahman |
| **Vishishtadvaita** (विशिष्टाद्वैत) | Ramanujacharya (11th c.) | Qualified non-duality: Brahman has real attributes | Organic dependence — Atman is real, part of Brahman's body |
| **Dvaita** (द्वैत) | Madhvacharya (13th c.) | Duality: eternal distinction between souls and God | Permanent distinction — Atman is eternally different from Brahman |

[Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

## Core Principles

**Brahman** — Ultimate reality characterised as Sat-Chit-Ananda (सत्-चित्-आनन्द, Being-Consciousness-Bliss). The non-dual ground of all existence. [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

**Maya/Avidya** — The cosmic/individual ignorance that creates the appearance of separation and multiplicity. See [[maya-avidya-cognitive-framework]] for the full cognitive distortion analysis. [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

**Pancha Kosha** — The five sheaths (Annamaya through Anandamaya) from the Taittiriya Upanishad, covering the Atman like layers of an onion. This is NeoRishi's "Being" layer. See [[pancha-kosha]]. [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

**Liberation** — Achievable through three paths: Jnana (knowledge, see [[yoga-paths-overview]]), Bhakti (devotion, see [[yoga-paths-overview]]), or Karma (selfless action, see [[karma-yoga]]). [Source: Vedanta_Darshana.md]

## Indian Philosophy — Overview and Heterodox Schools

Indian philosophy comprises six Astika (orthodox) Darshanas (दर्शन, "viewpoints") that accept the authority of the Vedas, and several Nastika (heterodox) schools that do not. Together, they form one of the richest philosophical traditions in human history, covering epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, psychology, and soteriology. This overview serves as a concept map for NeoRishi's educational content strategy. [Source: Indian_Philosophy_Reel_Topics.md]

## The Six Orthodox Schools (Shad Darshana)

| Darshana | Focus | Core Question | Key Text |
|----------|-------|---------------|----------|
| **Nyaya** (न्याय) | Logic and reasoning | How do we know what is true? | Nyaya Sutras (Gautama) |
| **Vaisheshika** (वैशेषिक) | Atomistic ontology | What is the universe made of? | Vaisheshika Sutras (Kanada) |
| **Sankhya** (सांख्य) | Dualistic metaphysics | What is the relationship between consciousness (Purusha) and matter (Prakriti)? | Sankhya Karika (Ishvara Krishna) |
| **Yoga** (योग) | Self-realisation through practice | How do we achieve liberation through discipline? | Yoga Sutras (Patanjali) |
| **Mimamsa** (मीमांसा) | Vedic hermeneutics | How do we correctly interpret sacred texts for action? | Mimamsa Sutras (Jaimini) |
| **Vedanta** (वेदान्त) | Ultimate reality | What is the nature of Brahman and its relation to Atman? | Brahma Sutras, Upanishads |

### Paired Traditions
These schools traditionally pair: **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** (logic + ontology), **Sankhya-Yoga** (theory + practice), **Mimamsa-Vedanta** (ritual interpretation + metaphysical culmination). [Source: Indian_Philosophy_Reel_Topics.md]

## Heterodox Schools (Nastika)

| School | Core Teaching |
|--------|--------------|
| **Charvaka** (चार्वाक) | Materialistic philosophy; rejects metaphysics and afterlife. Only perception is valid knowledge. |
| **Jainism** (जैन) | Non-violence (Ahimsa), non-absolutism (Anekantavada), spiritual liberation through austerity |
| **Buddhism** (बौद्ध) | Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, emptiness (Shunyata), liberation from suffering |

[Source: Indian_Philosophy_Reel_Topics.md]
