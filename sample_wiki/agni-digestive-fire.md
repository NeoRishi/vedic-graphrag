Agni (digestive/metabolic fire) is the master upstream variable in Ayurvedic pathophysiology. Its impairment is the single most consequential event in the disease cascade — Charaka treats Agni restoration as therapeutic priority #1 in almost all protocols. Agni operates at three hierarchical levels and exists in four constitutional types determined by Dosha influence. When impaired, Agni produces Ama (metabolic toxin), which accelerates all [[dosha-dushya-sammurchana|Dosha-Dushya interactions]] and clogs the body's channel system (Srotas). [Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md, Pachaka_Pitta.md, Samana_Vata.md]

## Three Levels of Agni

### 1. Jatharagni (Central Digestive Fire)
The master fire. Governs primary digestion in the stomach and small intestine. Represented by [[dosha-subtypes|Pachaka Pitta]] working in concert with [[dosha-subtypes|Samana Vata]] (kinetic energy) and [[dosha-subtypes|Kledaka Kapha]] (protective moisture). Its impairment is the master upstream cause of Ama formation, initiating the entire [[shatkriyakala|Sanchaya → Bheda cascade]].

### 2. Bhutagni (Five Elemental Fires)
Govern the conversion of digested food into element-specific nutrients (Akasha, Vayu, Agni, Jala, Prithvi). Their impairment produces element-specific tissue deficiencies.

### 3. Dhatvagni (Seven Tissue-Level Fires)
Each of the seven Dhatus (Rasa, Rakta, Mamsa, Meda, Asthi, Majja, Shukra) has its own metabolic fire. Impaired Dhatvagni at any level weakens that specific tissue, creating the Kha-Vaigunya (vulnerability) that attracts vitiated Doshas.

**Cascade hierarchy**: Jatharagni impairment → propagates through Bhutagni → to Dhatvagni → creating cascading tissue vulnerability. [Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Four Agni Types (Determined by Dosha)

| Agni Type | Dosha Influence | Characteristics | Ama Risk | Clinical Signs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sama Agni** | Balanced | Regular appetite, efficient digestion, stable energy | Low | Ideal digestive function |
| **Vishama Agni** | Vata-influenced | Fluctuating appetite, erratic digestion, gas, bloating | High | Irregular hunger, alternating constipation/loose stools |
| **Tikshna Agni** | Pitta-influenced | Intense hunger, rapid digestion, acid reflux, hypoglycemia | Moderate | Burns through food fast, irritable when meals skipped |
| **Manda Agni** | Kapha-influenced | Low appetite, slow digestion, heaviness, weight gain | High | Sluggish after meals, food "sits" in stomach |

[Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Ama — The Pathological Catalyst

When Agni is impaired, incomplete digestion produces Ama — a toxic, sticky, immunoreactive metabolic residue. Ama is not a single substance but a category: **any incompletely processed material at any metabolic level**.

**Ama accelerates pathology by:**
1. **Clogging Srotas** (channels) — restricting nutrient flow, creating local tissue hypoxia → conditions for Kha-Vaigunya
2. **Coating tissues** — reducing metabolic responsiveness and immune surveillance
3. **Combining with Doshas** to form **Sama states** (Sama Vata, Sama Pitta, Sama Kapha) — complexes that are heavier, stickier, and more pathogenic than either Ama or vitiated Dosha alone

> BRIDGE: Ama maps to multiple modern concepts simultaneously — circulating immune complexes, advanced glycation end products (AGEs), oxidized LDL, endotoxins from gut dysbiosis, and accumulated metabolic waste from impaired autophagy. [Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md]

> BRIDGE: Jatharagni impairment → Ama → systemic disease ↔ Modern pathway: ultra-processed food → gut dysbiosis → increased intestinal permeability → systemic endotoxemia → multi-organ inflammation → metabolic syndrome. [Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Agni and Daily Rhythm

Agni follows [[tridosha-overview|Dosha circadian rhythms]]:
- **Strongest**: 10 AM - 2 PM (Pitta time) — main meal should be eaten here
- **Variable**: 2-6 PM (Vata time) — light snack if needed
- **Lower**: 6-10 PM (Kapha time) — light dinner, eaten early

[Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md, Tridosha_Principle_Comprehensive_Guide.md]

## Therapeutic Priority

Agni assessment determines the **first therapeutic action**:
- If Agni is impaired → **Deepana-Pachana** (appetite-stimulating, Ama-digesting) therapy BEFORE any other intervention
- All dietary, herbal, and lifestyle interventions are modified by current Agni status
- No amount of "correct" food helps if Agni cannot process it

[Source: dosha_dushya_framework.md]

## Agni Across the Seasons

Agni varies dramatically across the six Ritus — this variation is the metabolic logic behind all seasonal dietary shifts:
- **Peak:** [[hemanta-ritu]] (Early Winter) — concentrated internal fire; heavy foods required
- **High:** [[shishira-ritu]] (Late Winter) — still strong but beginning decline
- **Declining:** [[vasanta-ritu]] (Spring) — Kapha dampens the flame
- **Weakest:** [[grishma-ritu]] (Summer) — thermoregulation diverts metabolic resources
- **Lowest:** [[varsha-ritu]] (Monsoon) — atmospheric instability fragments digestive capacity
- **Recovering:** [[sharad-ritu]] (Autumn) — gradually strengthening as humidity clears
